## Product, AI and delegation

ManyOsirisChat connects a business's Instagram account, Facebook Page and WhatsApp
number for business messaging and customer support. Optional AI is used for that
business's customer support only. Replies may be generated by AI and may be
inaccurate. The business must disclose AI replies to customers and provide human
help when appropriate. The policy pages disclose AI processing; automatic
per-message AI labeling is not claimed. Verify the customer-facing disclosure
in the recordings and supply the owner-approved wording.

Many retains Meta OAuth, encrypted Meta tokens, provider grant proof, raw Meta
webhooks, channel checks and Meta delivery. With delegation enabled for a channel,
Many forwards the normalized signed contract events to the SaaS chatbot. The SaaS
processes messages as a processor for the connected business and owns bot reply
generation, knowledge, inbox and handoff. It sends replies back through Many;
it does not receive Meta tokens or take over Meta webhook handling. Delegation
is off by default. Many AI does not provide a fallback for delegated replies.
See CONTRACT-V1.md and its adopted A16/v1.1 clauses under docs/saas-delegation.

Free-form support and AI replies use the applicable 24-hour messaging window;
the human-agent tag is NOT used. WhatsApp also honors a recorded valid free-entry
window in the current code. Outside the applicable window the local guard records
`window_closed` and makes no Meta send. Approved WhatsApp template outreach is a
separate consent/template workflow; it does not extend the ordinary AI window.
A queued reply or Many 202 response is not proof of Meta delivery.

Evidence: `backend/app/services/saas_outbound.py` (`_window_open`, `_deliver`),
`whatsapp_window_service.py`, `backend/tests/test_saas_messages.py`
(`test_closed_window_fails_without_sending`, `test_whatsapp_free_entry_window_counts_as_open`),
and the native Meta send clients. These existing code/tests support the wording;

## OAuth and Meta callbacks after R1

R1 stores encrypted typed provider context and subject plus an app/namespace/subject
HMAC lookup. Facebook retains its verified debug-token user and returned Page
grants. WhatsApp verifies app, WABA and returned phone membership. Instagram
retains its exchange subject and distinct returned profile context. Invalid apps,
missing subjects or mismatched grants fail before account/token/mapping commit.
Reconnect replaces the current grant; erasure-pending accounts cannot reconnect.
No account-ID backfill of legacy grants is performed.

Only a proven `app_user` namespace can match a Meta callback subject. WhatsApp
Embedded Signup and Instagram subjects remain `unresolved` under the approved
policy: provider context is stored, but no equivalence to signed_request.user_id
is inferred from a Page/WABA/phone/profile/business ID or browser input. Unresolved
grants and unproven callback identities go to the operator ledger. An operator
must verify both subject namespace and channel grant before explicit mapping.

Source callback paths (prepend [OWNER: verified Many API public base URL]):

- `POST /meta/compliance/instagram/deauthorize`
- `POST /meta/compliance/instagram/data-deletion`
- `POST /meta/compliance/facebook/deauthorize` (Facebook Pages and WhatsApp app)
- `POST /meta/compliance/facebook/data-deletion`
- Returned status path: `/meta/data-deletion/status/{confirmation_code}`.

Callbacks validate bounded form-encoded HMAC-SHA256 signed requests with the
configured app secret. Verified deauthorization returns 200, including an unknown
subject, and replays reuse a durable receipt. A proven match removes its tokens,
disconnects the channel, disables AI/delegation and drops ordinary pending forwards.
A16 erasure work survives. Unproven subjects cause no guessed disconnection.
Data-deletion callbacks return an opaque confirmation code and status URL; the
page remains pending until the operator confirms local AND SaaS completion.
Unknown receipt codes return 404. Acceptance never marks deletion complete.

Evidence: `meta_grant_identity.py`, `meta_compliance.py`,
`backend/app/api/routes_meta_compliance.py`, `test_r1_oauth.py`,
`test_meta_compliance.py` and `test_account_erasure.py`. R1's exact commands and
limits are in [R1-IMPLEMENTATION.md](saas-delegation/R1-IMPLEMENTATION.md).

## A16 and operator deletion

A16 `contact.erasure` uses the existing signed event contract. Its minimal envelope
contains the same channel identity used for customer messages; no message body,
separate name/phone, tenant ID or bot ID is added. The authenticated connection
determines the SaaS tenant. HTTP 202 means durably accepted, including duplicate
acceptance; it does NOT establish worker completion. Revoked connections or failed
work require operator remediation.

Owners can start `DELETE /facebook/accounts/{id}/hard` and the WhatsApp equivalent.
They receive 202 and an opaque job ID; retained-job replays reuse it and another
workspace receives 404. Phase 1 locks the channel, marks erasure pending, removes
Meta tokens, disables AI/delegation/outreach, queues/reuses applicable A16 requests
and erases scoped customer data in one transaction. It includes queued-only
identities and WhatsApp contacts without leads. Signing configuration stays in
the disabled account until remote acceptance or verified operator cleanup.

Phase 2 removes remaining account rows and the authorization map in one transaction
only after every linked A16 has exact 202 acceptance or the operator independently
verifies SaaS cleanup. The public receipt still needs separate verified completion.
Missing signing configuration, unknown historical scope, legacy unproven acceptance,
failed work or unscoped audit text remain visible operator cases. The existing
Instagram immediate hard-delete route is unchanged: coordinate customer/A16 erasure
and remote cleanup before deleting a delegated Instagram account.

Operators verify requester, app namespace, workspace and scope, quiesce in-flight
work, check Many removal and tenant-scoped SaaS erasure/payload cleanup, preserve
sanitized evidence and only then attest completion. A deletion cannot recall a
message already sent or erase a worker's in-flight memory. Commands and failure
handling: [operator runbook](data-deletion-runbook.md). Do not present local tests
as deployed cross-application erasure evidence.

## Retention and public policies

Public source pages: `/privacy`, `/terms`, and `/privacy#data-deletion`. Public
availability, contacts and effective date need owner confirmation. The policy
text identifies the SaaS processor, AI replies, separate stored copies and the
operator verification requirement.

Many has no universal automatic expiry for all customer conversations. Its raw
webhook purge uses 30 days. Ordinary successful/dropped forward payloads are
cleared; failed ordinary payloads clear after 7 days and ordinary terminal/outbound
idempotency rows after 30 days. Pending/failed A16 work is retained for remediation.
Minimal account jobs are purged only 30 days after independently verified remote
cleanup; callback receipts have no universal automatic expiry. SaaS event payloads
follow tenant message retention and its dedupe records remain at least 30 days.
Configured jobs running in deployment, backups and owner-approved retention terms
must be verified before making service commitments.

Evidence: `contact_erasure.py`, `account_erasure.py`, `saas_forwarder.py`, their
retention tests, and the adopted contract. No automatic 30-day deletion guarantee,
verified deployment region, subprocessor list or legal contact is invented here.
